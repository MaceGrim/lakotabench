LakotaBench

Taŋyáŋ yahí.

AI companies say their models speak every language.
Let’s find out what they know about Lakȟótiyapi.

LakotaBench is a community-governed measure of what language models actually understand about the Lakota language — built question by question with Lakota language experts, on the community’s terms.

How it works

Your words stay yours

  • Kept exactly as you wrote them. Whatever spelling you use, we keep your original writing untouched, always.
  • You decide what becomes public. Questions stay private unless the community chooses otherwise.
  • Never used to train AI. Your contributions test the machines — they do not teach them — unless the community decides differently, together.
  • Withdraw anything, any time. Ask, and it comes out. No questions, no delay.

How it works

  1. 1

    Write questions

    You write questions in Lakota the way you know it — words, grammar, full sentences — along with the right answer and a few wrong ones a learner might pick.

  2. 2

    Review each other

    Another speaker answers your question without seeing your answer first. When fluent speakers agree, we know the question is solid.

  3. 3

    Hold AI accountable

    We put the questions to the AI models and publish what they got right — and what they got wrong. The world sees the evidence.

Why measure?

The best way to learn Lakȟótiyapi has never changed: time with speakers, in family and community. No technology replaces that, and none ever will. But the right tools, honestly judged, can back up the people doing the work — giving teachers more reach, giving learners a way to practice between lessons, so that every hour spent face-to-face counts for more.

That only works if the tools actually know the language. AI is already in learners’ pockets, answering questions about Lakota whether or not it knows it — and right now, no one can say what it gets wrong. LakotaBench gives the community the evidence: which tools are fit to stand alongside a teacher, which ones fail, and proof of the gap the AI companies need to close.

The language lives in people. This makes sure the machines serve them.
